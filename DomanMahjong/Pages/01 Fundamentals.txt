OVERVIEW
Riichi Mahjong (or Doman Mahjong) is a four-player Japanese mahjong game. The aim of each hand is to complete a legal winning hand with at least one yaku (a scoring pattern), then make decisions that improve your final placement over the full game.

THE BASIC RHYTHM
1. Receive your starting tiles.
2. On your turn, draw one tile (unless a call changes the flow), then discard one tile.
3. Improve your shape while keeping a route to a yaku.
4. Win by tsumo (drawing your winning tile yourself) or ron (winning on another player's discard).

THE STANDARD WINNING SHAPE
A standard hand is usually made of four groups of three tiles and a pair of identical tiles. A group can be a sequence or a triplet; a quad is also possible after a kan.

Two important exceptions are Chiitoitsu (Seven Pairs) and Kokushi Musou (Thirteen Orphans). They do not use the standard four-groups-and-a-pair structure.

THREE QUESTIONS TO ASK EACH TURN
1. What improves my hand? Prefer shapes that accept more useful tiles. This is tile efficiency.
2. Can this hand score? Identify a yaku plan; dora alone does not make a hand legal.
3. Is it safe to continue? When an opponent threatens to win, compare your hand's value and progress against the risk of dealing in.

BEGINNER PRIORITIES
- Learn the tile families and recognize sequences, triplets, and pairs at a glance.
- Track your hand's distance from ready: tenpai means one tile away from winning.
- Keep a balance between speed, hand value, and safety.
- Remember that the goal of a full game is a good final placement, not simply to win as many individual hands as possible.
