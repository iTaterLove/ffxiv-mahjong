THREE STEPS TO A SCORE
1. Count han: add the han from each yaku, then add bonus han from dora, red dora, and (when eligible) ura dora.
2. Determine fu: fu come from the base hand, winning method, concealed ron, group types, value-tile pair, and certain waits. Some hands use fixed fu values.
3. Apply limits and payment rules: use the score table for the han/fu combination, whether the winner is dealer, and whether the win is ron or tsumo.

COMMON FU REFERENCE
| Source | Fu added |
| --- | --- |
| Base for standard hands | 20 fu |
| Tsumo (except pinfu) | +2 fu |
| Closed hand winning by ron | +10 fu |
| Open hand winning by ron | +0 fu |
| Open triplet of simples | +2 fu |
| Concealed triplet of simples | +4 fu |
| Open triplet of terminals/honors | +4 fu |
| Concealed triplet of terminals/honors | +8 fu |
| Open quad of simples / terminals-honors | +8 / +16 fu |
| Concealed quad of simples / terminals-honors | +16 / +32 fu |
| Pair of dragons, seat wind, or prevailing wind | +2 fu for each applicable value |
| Closed, edge, or single wait | +2 fu |

Fu is not simply added in every exceptional hand. Pinfu and Seven Pairs have special scoring treatment; ordinary fu rounding and exceptions apply. This table is a learning summary, not a complete scoring algorithm.

LIMIT HANDS
When a hand reaches a limit, use the limit-hand payment rather than continuing ordinary han/fu multiplication. Familiarize yourself with mangan and higher limits, including haneman, baiman, sanbaiman, and yakuman. Multiple yakuman and double-yakuman treatment depend on the rules in use.

RON VERSUS TSUMO
- Ron: the player who discarded the winning tile pays the hand's ron value.
- Tsumo: the other players pay according to the dealer/non-dealer payment schedule; the dealer's share differs from a non-dealer's.
- Dealer win: dealer wins generally have higher payments than an equivalent non-dealer win.
- Riichi bet: the 1,000-point stick is awarded according to the applicable end-of-hand rules.
- Honba/counters: counters add payments according to the rule set.

END-OF-GAME POINTS
Keep raw points, placement bonuses (uma), and any winning premium (oka) conceptually separate. The book describes a Tenhou scoring setup with oka and 10–20 uma; other formats, including EMA events, may use different end-of-game calculations.
