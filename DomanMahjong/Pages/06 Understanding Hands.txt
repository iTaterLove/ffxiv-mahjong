RECOGNIZE THE BUILDING BLOCKS
| Shape | Example | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sequence / run | 3-4-5 in one suit | Three consecutive numbered tiles in the same suit. |
| Triplet | 7-7-7 | Three identical tiles. |
| Pair / head | East-East | Two identical tiles; standard hands need one pair. |
| Side-wait protorun | 4-5 | Can complete as 3-4-5 or 4-5-6. |
| Closed-wait protorun | 4-6 | Needs the middle tile, 5. |
| Edge-wait protorun | 1-2 or 8-9 | Needs 3 or 7 respectively. |
| Floating tile | East alone | Not yet part of a useful group; it may connect later or be discarded. |

THE FIVE-BLOCK METHOD
1. Divide the hand into blocks. Look for groups, protoruns, pairs, and useful shapes.
2. Count the blocks. A standard hand ultimately needs five components: four groups plus one head.
3. Find redundancy. If you have more than five plausible blocks, compare which one contributes least to speed or value.
4. Build missing blocks. If you have fewer than five, keep the floating tiles most likely to connect.
5. Consider yaku and safety. The tile that maximizes immediate tile acceptance is not always best if another discard preserves a realistic scoring route or avoids danger.

WAIT TYPES
- Side wait (ryanmen): two-sided wait, such as 3-4 waiting on 2 or 5. Usually the most flexible incomplete sequence shape.
- Closed wait (kanchan): gap in the middle, such as 3-5 waiting on 4.
- Edge wait (penchan): 1-2 waiting on 3 or 8-9 waiting on 7.
- Pair wait (tanki): waiting for the matching tile to complete the pair.
- Dual pon wait (shanpon): two pairs; either matching tile can complete a triplet.
- Single wait: only one tile type completes the hand, such as a pair wait or a closed/edge wait.

WHAT IS A YAKU?
A yaku is the scoring condition that makes a hand legal to win. Common examples introduced in the book include:
- Riichi: declare with a closed tenpai hand.
- Tanyao (All Simples): use only 2–8 numbered tiles.
- Yakuhai: make a triplet of dragons, seat wind, or prevailing wind.
- Pinfu: a closed hand with four sequences, a non-value pair, and a qualifying two-sided wait.
- Ittsuu (Pure Straight): sequences 123, 456, and 789 in one suit.
- Sanshoku (Mixed Triple Chow): the same sequence in all three suits.
- Honitsu (Half Flush): one numbered suit plus honors.
- Toitoi (All Pungs): four triplet/quad groups and a pair.
- Chiitoitsu (Seven Pairs): seven distinct pairs.

IMPORTANT
A hand can be tenpai but still lack a yaku. Before calling ron or tsumo, the hand must satisfy a legal yaku under the rules being played. Dora alone is not enough.
