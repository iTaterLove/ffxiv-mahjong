1. THE TILE SET
A standard four-player riichi set has 136 tiles:
- 108 suited tiles: three suits, numbered 1–9, four copies of each tile.
- 16 wind tiles: East, South, West, and North, four copies each.
- 12 dragon tiles: White, Green, and Red, four copies each.
- Red fives, when used, replace ordinary fives rather than adding extra tiles to the 136-tile set.
Flowers and seasons are not used in standard four-player riichi.

2. SETUP AND DEAL
The precise dice, wall-breaking, and dealing procedure should be learned from a rules diagram or a live demonstration, because small procedural errors can affect the dead wall and dora indicators.
At a high level:
1. Shuffle tiles face down and build four walls.
2. Determine the dealer and break the wall using the table's procedure.
3. Deal the starting tiles. The dealer begins with one extra tile and makes the first discard.
4. Reveal the initial dora indicator according to the rules.
5. Keep the dead wall and live wall distinct; replacement draws and dora indicators use designated dead-wall positions.

3. THE TURN CYCLE
A normal turn is draw one tile, evaluate the hand, discard one tile. Calls alter the flow:
- Chii claims a discard to complete a sequence and is normally limited to the player immediately before you in turn order.
- Pon claims a discard to complete a triplet.
- An open kan claims a discard to complete a quad.
- An added kan upgrades a previously called triplet by adding the fourth tile.
- A concealed kan uses four matching concealed tiles, subject to the applicable procedure.
- A kan normally causes a replacement draw and an additional dora indicator to be revealed according to the rules.
- Calls may interrupt ippatsu and can affect whether a first-turn/abortive-draw condition remains valid.

4. THE WALL AND DEAD WALL
The live wall supplies ordinary draws. The dead wall is reserved for dora indicators and replacement draws after kans. The exact indicator and replacement-draw procedure matters, especially after multiple kans. A table's official rules should specify the maximum number of kans and what happens if all four kans are declared by one player versus multiple players.

5. HANDS, ROUNDS, AND MATCHES
- A hand is one deal and its play until a win or draw.
- A round is identified by the prevailing wind, commonly East then South in a standard hanchan.
- A hanchan typically contains East and South rounds; an East-only game is shorter.
- Seat wind identifies a player's current seat role and can change as the dealer rotates.
- Dealer continuation commonly occurs after the dealer wins and may occur after a draw when the dealer is tenpai, depending on the rules.
- The match ends under the applicable round-completion and extension rules. Some games allow extra rounds if the required leader/score condition is not met.

6. DRAWS AND ABORTIVE DRAWS
An exhaustive draw occurs when the live wall is exhausted without a win. Players may reveal whether they are tenpai under the rules, and payments may be exchanged between tenpai and noten players.
Abortive draws end a hand early under specified conditions. Examples used in common rule sets include:
- Nine terminals and honors on a player's first turn.
- Four players declaring riichi.
- Three players winning on the same discard.
- Four identical wind tiles discarded during the first uninterrupted round.
- Four kans declared by different players.
Not every ruleset includes every abortive draw, and the exact timing requirements matter.

7. FURITEN: THE ESSENTIAL DISTINCTION
Furiten prevents a player from winning by ron on any tile that completes their current wait when the furiten condition applies. It does not normally prevent a self-draw win.
Common categories:
- Own-discard furiten: one or more tiles in the current winning wait are in your own discard row.
- Temporary furiten: you pass a legal ron opportunity and are temporarily unable to ron until the relevant reset, commonly your next draw.
- Riichi furiten: after passing a legal ron opportunity while in riichi, the furiten restriction lasts for the remainder of the hand under common rules.
Furiten applies to the wait as a whole, not only to the particular tile previously discarded. Learn this rule before playing online or at a physical table.
